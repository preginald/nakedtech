# Naked Tech article visual standard

Owner: MSOS Editorial, with Codex/Engineering implementing and verifying. Approved by Peter after the Wi-Fi rollout omitted the illustrated treatment. Applies to every new or redesigned educational article; existing supporting articles must meet it during their planned redesign, not silently count as accepted.

- Include at least one useful explanatory image, illustration or substantial diagram inside the article. Logos, decorative icons, tables alone and social-preview metadata do not satisfy visual coverage.
- Match the established Slow Computer visual rhythm: illustrations belong inside symptom cards by default, using the shared proportions, spacing and rounded treatment. A pillar does not need oversized standalone images or an image in every section. Use a separate diagram only when it materially explains the topic. Relevance and consistent scale take priority over image count.
- Prefer real explanatory diagrams, accurate screenshots or topic-specific illustrations. No filler stock imagery, fabricated screenshots represented as actual software, or images that imply unverified diagnoses.
- Follow Naked Tech colours and visual language. Inspect light and dark themes on desktop and mobile. Dark mode must avoid large bright panels; a deliberately dark illustration may be used in both themes when it remains clear and visually integrated.
- Keep instructions and essential labels in HTML. Supply meaningful alt text for informative images and captions when interpretation matters. Reserve dimensions, compress assets, lazy-load below-fold images and check crops at mobile widths.
- Editorial owns relevance, clarity and factual fidelity. Engineering checks loading, dimensions, accessibility markup and layout. Publication evidence must name the inspected visuals and record screenshots; a passing structural test is not visual approval.

Current rollout: Slow Computer and Wi-Fi pillars and the slow-startup and computer-or-internet and Wi-Fi two-device supporting guides use the standard. The remaining seven supporting articles still require adaptation and visual review. The site audit enforces image coverage for the adopted guides; extend that coverage when each supporting article is redesigned. This is an explicit rollout gap, not a claim that all existing pages already comply.
