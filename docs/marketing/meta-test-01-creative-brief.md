## Controlled creative system

The experiment should compare the two customer pains, not two unrelated art directions. Hold these variables constant:

- **Format:** one static single-image concept per pain.
- **Primary master:** `1080 × 1350` (`4:5`) for feed placements.
- **Square derivative:** a separately checked `1080 × 1080` (`1:1`) crop.
- **Vertical placements:** use a separately inspected `1080 × 1920` (`9:16`) composition rather than accepting an automatic crop.
- **Horizontal placements:** use a separately inspected `1200 × 628` (`1.91:1`) composition rather than accepting an automatic crop.
- **Visual system:** warm, believable Ivanhoe/Eaglemont home setting; charcoal brand field or gradient; electric-peach accent; bone-white type; one concise hook and one supporting line.
- **Composition:** keep the problem subject and essential text inside the centred square-safe area so neither feed crop loses the message.
- **Copy structure:** pain in the first line, diagnosis/decision promise next, approved local offer last.
- **Local relevance:** Ivanhoe and Eaglemont only.
- **CTA:** `Learn More` for both concepts.
- **No extra variable:** no alternate headline, carousel, testimonial, catalogue of services or second offer in the initial comparison.

## Concept A — Wi-Fi dropouts

### Strategic job

Let a local resident recognise one persistent problem: Wi-Fi works near the router but becomes unreliable in rooms where it is actually used. Continue directly into a diagnostic-first landing page rather than selling Mesh or another booster.

### Message contract

| Field | Approved brief direction |
|---|---|
| Pain | Unreliable Wi-Fi in the bedroom, study or another regularly used room |
| Promise | Diagnose the NBN service, router, placement, interference and coverage before recommending hardware |
| Offer | `$190 fixed-price Wi-Fi dropout diagnosis`, including local call-out in Ivanhoe or Eaglemont; no Mesh purchase required |
| Landing-page match | `Wi-Fi keeps dropping out? Find the cause before buying more hardware.` |
| CTA | `Learn More` |
| Destination | `https://nakedtech.au/services/wifi-dropouts-ivanhoe/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=naked_tech_pain_points_01&utm_content=wifi_dropouts_v1` |

### Draft ad copy

**Primary text**

> Wi-Fi that works beside the router but drops out in the rooms where it is needed calls for a diagnosis—not another guessed-at booster.
>
> Naked Tech checks the NBN service, router, placement, interference and coverage, then explains the right next step in plain English.
>
> $190 fixed-price Wi-Fi dropout diagnosis, including local call-out in Ivanhoe or Eaglemont. Mesh only if the evidence shows it is needed.

**Headline**

> Wi-Fi keeps dropping out? Find the cause.

**Description**

> $190 fixed-price diagnosis · Ivanhoe & Eaglemont

**On-image copy**

> WI-FI KEEPS DROPPING OUT?<br>
> Find the cause before buying more hardware.

### Visual problem

Show a recognisable distance-and-room problem, not a generic technician or equipment catalogue. The recommended scene is a warm, lived-in home with the router visible in one room and a laptop or everyday device losing connection in a study or bedroom beyond it. Use a restrained, non-branded connection indicator or subtle signal fall-off to make the dropout legible without turning the image into a technical diagram.

The scene must feel physically plausible: one coherent daylight direction, matching shadows and colour temperature, realistic device scale, and no impossible signal graphics passing through objects. Avoid an exaggerated distressed person; the frustration should come from the situation.

### Crop and layout

- In `4:5`, reserve the upper portion for the two-line hook and keep the router-to-room relationship visible below.
- Keep both the device and the point of signal loss within the centred `1:1` safe crop.
- The electric-peach accent may trace one short interrupted signal path or keyline; it must not become decorative noise.
- Do not place text over a busy shelf, window or light source.

### Existing-asset decision

- `src/img/services-hero.webp` shows a person moving a small smart-speaker-style device. It can be mistaken for the router and does not clearly show a dropout. **Reject as the primary ad image.**
- `src/img/toolkit-flatlay.webp` is a generic equipment catalogue and weakens the one-pain message. **Reject.**
- `src/img/nakedtech_hero_technician.webp` is friendly brand imagery but depicts a solution state rather than the Wi-Fi problem. **Do not use for the initial concept.**

**Recommendation:** create a new, problem-specific primary image after this brief is approved.

**Pre-launch watchpoint:** the Wi-Fi landing page currently uses `services-hero.webp` as its Open Graph fallback. Leave it unchanged before creative approval; once the final asset is approved, decide explicitly whether the destination preview should adopt it and verify the resulting Meta preview before launch.

### Prohibited drift

- Do not list home security, home-office setup, computer support or unrelated services.
- Do not lead with Mesh, a router sale or a whole-home installation price.
- Do not guarantee that the diagnosis fixes an ISP, NBN or third-party fault.
- Do not state or imply that Meta has revealed a viewer's private home-network condition.

## Concept B — Slow computer / Windows decision

### Strategic job

Let a local resident recognise one persistent problem: a Windows computer is chronically slow and the sensible choice among fixing, upgrading or replacing it is unclear. Continue directly into diagnosis and decision support rather than using fear about malware or Windows 10.

### Message contract

| Field | Approved brief direction |
|---|---|
| Pain | A chronically slow Windows computer and uncertainty about whether spending more on it is worthwhile |
| Promise | Provide a 60–75 minute onsite assessment, complete safe straightforward fixes within scope and deliver a written fix, upgrade or replace plan |
| Offer | **`$190 fixed-price` Slow Computer Performance Assessment**; local call-out included in Ivanhoe or Eaglemont; workshop remediation and follow-up are separately quoted |
| Landing-page match | `Computer painfully slow? Find out whether to fix, upgrade or replace.` |
| CTA | `Learn More` |
| Destination | `https://nakedtech.au/services/slow-computer-help-ivanhoe/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=naked_tech_pain_points_01&utm_content=slow_computer_v1` |

### Draft ad copy

**Primary text**

> A Windows computer that takes forever to start, freezes or makes ordinary tasks a wait needs an evidence-based decision—not guesswork.
>
> Naked Tech's $190 fixed-price onsite assessment gives you 60–75 minutes of diagnosis, safe straightforward fixes within scope, a Windows compatibility review and a written fix, upgrade or replace plan.
>
> Peter personally brings more than 30 years of technology and support experience. Local call-out is included in Ivanhoe or Eaglemont; any workshop or follow-up work is quoted separately.

**Headline**

> Slow computer? Fix, upgrade or replace.

**Description**

> $190 fixed · 60–75 min onsite · Ivanhoe & Eaglemont

**On-image copy**

> SLOW COMPUTER?<br>
> Clear diagnosis before you spend.

### Visual problem

Show an intact, unbranded laptop in a real home setting, visibly stalled during an ordinary task. A restrained loading indicator communicates slowness; a mug and reading glasses may imply a resident without showing a person or generated anatomy. Avoid broken-screen imagery, panic, fake virus warnings or a staged repair bench. The image should pose the problem. The copy carries the repair/upgrade/replace decision.

Use the same lighting, brand field, typography, title scale and crop logic as Concept A so the comparison remains fair. Do not use a recognisable Microsoft screenshot or third-party brand interface; a neutral, plausible desktop state is sufficient.

### Crop and layout

- In `4:5`, keep the laptop screen, loading ring and hook visible without the UI becoming illegible.
- Compose the laptop and loading ring inside the centred `1:1` safe crop.
- In `9:16`, preserve the complete laptop and loading ring; peripheral mug, glasses and books may be deliberately removed by the approved crop.
- In `1.91:1`, use the untreated full-bleed photograph with a narrower left typography column; do not add a grey panel, gradient or fade.
- Preserve enough calm negative space for the hook; do not cover the screen with a large badge.
- Keep the visual treatment human and domestic rather than forensic or workshop-like.

### Existing-asset decision

- `src/img/slow-computer-help-og.webp` has strong message hierarchy and correct landing-page language, but its `1200 × 630` split composition does not survive `4:5` or `1:1` cropping. **Use as a typography/brand reference or backup link card, not as the feed master.**
- `src/img/nakedtech_hero_technician.webp` provides the source photograph used by the OG card, but the smiling technician and generic laptop depict a solution state rather than chronic slowness. **Do not use unchanged for the initial ad.**
- `src/img/toolkit-flatlay.webp` reads as a broad repair catalogue. **Reject.**

**Recommendation:** create a new problem-specific primary image after this brief is approved. If a rapid fallback is later required, redesign the existing OG direction natively for `4:5`; do not crop it automatically.

### Prohibited drift

- Do not imply every slow computer has malware or a security breach.
- Do not use Windows 10 end-of-support as a fear device or force a replacement conclusion.
- Do not promise a one-visit fix, drift back to `from $190`, or imply parts, account recovery, provider work, data transfer, new-computer setup, workshop remediation or follow-up are included.
- Do not list Wi-Fi, printers, email, phones or unrelated services.
- Do not state or imply that Meta has revealed a viewer's device condition, age or security status.
